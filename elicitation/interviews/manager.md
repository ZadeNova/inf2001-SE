# **Part A: Manager interview**

## **Opening**

**INTERVIEWER:  Hi, thank you for making time for this interview. We are the project team building the new job allocation and workforce system for the company. This session is being recorded and transcribed on Zoom so we can capture your requirements accurately. Is that okay with you?**

**MANAGER:**  Yes, that's fine. Go ahead.

**INTERVIEWER:  Great. Could you briefly introduce your role?**

**MANAGER:**  Sure. I'm the operations manager. I handle the weekly roster for our six drivers and eleven technicians, I take in customer jobs, allocate them to the vans, and approve leave. Right now most of that is done on spreadsheets and WhatsApp, which is why we want a proper system.

## **Main interview**

**INTERVIEWER:  Could you walk me through the full schedule planning process?**   *[Process Q1]*

**MANAGER:**  Every week follows the same cycle. Staff update their availability by 6pm on Wednesday. On Thursday I start planning. First I check which jobs are booked and which vans are available, because vans might be at the workshop. Then I look at who is available each day. I build the crews first, and then I allocate the jobs to each van. On Monday I release the schedule, and it covers the following week, Monday to Saturday. So staff always get a week's notice.

**INTERVIEWER:  In what order or priority do you arrange the staff? For example by staff preferences, by available vans, drivers first, or dual-brand or single-brand technicians first?**   *[Process Q1 follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  Vans first, because that's the hard limit. After that I try to group jobs in the same area and put a van in that area. Next I set the van crew. I prioritise the two dual-certified technicians, because they are the scarcest. I put them on the vans that have jobs for both brands. After that I fill the single-brand vans with M Electric or Dicon technicians, and then I pair a driver with each van. Their preferences come last. I use them as a tie-breaker, not a rule. I also try to balance hours, so extra jobs go to whoever has the lowest workload that week.

**INTERVIEWER:  When a job is done, what information do you require the technicians to send over?**   *[Process Q1 follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  They should mark the job as completed in the system with a photo of the invoice signed by the customer. The invoice has the start and finish time, a short remark on what was done, and whether a follow-up visit is needed, for example if a part has to be ordered.

**INTERVIEWER:  How does a customer request become a job that is ready for allocation? What information do you need?**   *[Process Q2]*

**MANAGER:**  Customers call or message our office, and I enter the job myself. There's no other system to import from. Today it's all in a spreadsheet. For each job I need the customer's name and phone number, the address with postal code, the brand, whether it's an installation or servicing, the number of units, the aircon model if they know it, and their preferred date, morning or afternoon. If a customer has units of both brands, I log it as two jobs at the same address and put both on a mixed-brand van. Once a job has all that, it's ready to allocate.

**INTERVIEWER:  Do the hours vary each time, or are they generally fixed?**   *[Process Q2 follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  We use standard durations. Servicing is one hour per unit, and installation is three hours per unit. I'd like the system to fill those in automatically, but let me adjust them, for example if an installation is on a higher floor and needs extra piping.

**INTERVIEWER:  How far in advance do you accept jobs?**   *[Process Q2 follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  We don’t have a specific limit, but usually customers book 1-2 weeks in advance, we rarely get bookings more than 4 weeks in advance.

**INTERVIEWER:  And how are customer changes or cancellations handled?**   *[added follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  The customer calls me, and I update or cancel the job in the system. If it's already allocated, the crew should see the change straight away and get a notification.

**INTERVIEWER:  How is the servicing of vans planned and recorded?**   *[Process Q3]*

**MANAGER:**  It's a fixed rotation. Every van goes to the workshop every two months, on the same date. In odd months, Van 1 goes on the 1st, Van 2 on the 11th and Van 3 on the 21st. In even months it's Vans 4, 5 and 6 on the same dates. That way we never have more than one van out at a time. On service days we have five vans, and every other day all six. If a date falls on a Sunday or public holiday, it moves to the next working day. The van is out for the whole day. I'd like the system to record these dates, so the van shows as unavailable and I can't allocate jobs to it that day.

**INTERVIEWER:  Walk us through how you review and approve staff leave.**   *[Process Q4]*

**MANAGER:**  Staff should apply for leave in the system, ideally at least two weeks ahead. I check the dates, make sure we can still run at least three vans that day, and then approve or reject. Once it's approved, those days should automatically show as unavailable, so I can't allocate them by mistake. Everyone gets seven days of annual leave per calendar year, and it doesn't carry over. The system should track the balance, so staff can see how many days they have left. Sick leave is separate. If someone is on MC, they call me and I mark them unavailable.

**INTERVIEWER:  Do staff need to provide reasons?**   *[Process Q4 follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  Not for annual leave, but they can add a note if they want. If I reject a request, I'll give a reason.

## **Clarifications**

**INTERVIEWER:  As a manager, do you have your own availability? Are you full-time?**   *[Clarification Q1]*

**MANAGER:**  I'm full-time and office-based. I don't go out on jobs, so I don't need to enter availability, and my hours shouldn't be counted in the workload. Workload tracking is only for drivers and technicians.

**INTERVIEWER:  Are you in charge of adding new staff, or is it the IT administrator?**   *[Clarification Q2]*

**MANAGER:**  The IT administrator creates the accounts for staff and managers. I just give them the details: name, contact number, role, and for technicians, which brands they're certified for.

**INTERVIEWER:  Do the certificates of the technicians or drivers need to be uploaded? What information is required?**   *[Clarification Q2 follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  For technicians, I need the brand, the certificate number and the expiry date. Certificates are renewed every two years, so the system should remind me a month before one expires. Uploading a scanned copy would be nice, but it isn't essential. Drivers don't need certificates in the system.

**INTERVIEWER:  Can technicians also be drivers?**   *[Clarification Q3]*

**MANAGER:**  No. Our drivers drive and our technicians do the technical work. Only the drivers are covered by the company vehicle insurance, so every van needs one of the six drivers.

**INTERVIEWER:  How flexible can employee hours be? For example half days, or certain hours like part time?**   *[Clarification Q4]*

**MANAGER:**  Everyone is full-time, but availability is given in half days. Morning is 9 to 1, and afternoon is 2 to 6. Lunch is 1 to 2. We don't need anything finer than that.

**INTERVIEWER:  Do the drivers and technicians assigned to each other stick together for the day, or is it flexible?**   *[Clarification Q4 follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  They stay together for the whole day, in the same van. Crews can change from one day to the next, but not in the middle of the day, unless there's an emergency and I move someone myself.

**INTERVIEWER:  The requirements say "servicing or installing". Are technicians qualified in both, or are they different services?**   *[Clarification Q5]*

**MANAGER:**  A brand certification covers both installation and servicing for that brand. So an M Electric technician can install and service M Electric units, but can't touch Dicon. The two dual-certified technicians can do both types of job for both brands.

**INTERVIEWER:  Then what would their allocated hours be? Is it fixed?**   *[Clarification Q5 follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  It follows the standard durations: one hour per unit for servicing and three hours per unit for installation. A technician's allocated hours are just the total of their jobs, plus travel.

**INTERVIEWER:  Since staff can add availability up to one month in advance, can you also set schedules up to one month in advance, or only for the following week?**   *[Clarification Q6]*

**MANAGER:**  I can view availability up to a month ahead, which helps me plan leave and big installations. But I only allocate and publish one week at a time.

**INTERVIEWER:  Does the schedule released on Monday cover that same week or the next one? And by what time on Wednesday must availability be in?**   *[Clarification Q6 follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  It covers the following week. For example, for the week starting Monday the 19th, availability is due by 6pm on Wednesday the 7th, I plan from Thursday the 8th, and I publish on Monday the 12th. After the Wednesday deadline, the system should lock that week's availability, and any change goes to me as a request that I approve case by case.

**INTERVIEWER:  How are employees' hours counted? Does travelling time or breaks count as hours?**   *[Clarification Q7]*

**MANAGER:**  Travel counts, breaks don't. The system won't know real travel times, so I'd like a fixed allowance of 30 minutes per job, added on top of the job duration. Lunch doesn't count. Drivers get the same hours as the van they're on, because they're with the crew the whole time, helping carry equipment. The week is Monday to Saturday. Forty hours is a normal full week, so anyone above forty hours is doing overtime, and that's what I want highlighted. The hours should show the planned time first, and once a job is completed, use the actual time instead.

**INTERVIEWER:  For the three staff with the lowest workload, should drivers and technicians be ranked together?**   *[added follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  No, separately. I want to see the three lowest technicians and the three lowest drivers, because I can't swap one for the other. Anyone on leave that week should be left out.

**INTERVIEWER:  What scheduling rules apply on Sundays and public holidays?**   *[Clarification Q8]*

**MANAGER:**  We're closed on Sundays and public holidays, so there are no jobs and no roster on those days. The three-van minimum only applies Monday to Saturday. The IT administrator should load the public holiday list once a year, so the system blocks those dates. Very rarely a customer has an emergency on a Sunday, but I handle that outside the system.

## **Conflicts and edge cases**

**INTERVIEWER:  In the event that there are not enough technicians, drivers or vans, how do you usually handle it?**   *[Conflicts Q1]*

**MANAGER:**  First I reshuffle. For example, I split a three-person van, or move a dual-certified technician onto a van that's short. If that's not enough, I ask available staff whether they can take extra hours. If we still can't run three vans, I postpone the lower-priority servicing jobs and call those customers. The system shouldn't stop me from publishing, but it should clearly warn me when a day has fewer than three vans, and show which jobs are still unassigned.

**INTERVIEWER:  How are availability changes handled?**   *[Conflicts Q2]*

**MANAGER:**  Before the Wednesday deadline, staff can change their availability freely. After the deadline, they submit a change request in the system with a reason, and I approve or reject it. If something happens after the schedule is published, like a family emergency, they should call me, and I update the system myself.

**INTERVIEWER:  Are there any staff on standby?**   *[Conflicts Q2 follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  No dedicated standby staff. But anyone who marked themselves available and didn't get allocated is effectively on standby, so I'd like the system to show me who that is.

**INTERVIEWER:  If there is a last-minute cancellation by a staff member, how will it be handled? Would you assign standby staff, or allow any staff to take up the open job through the system?**   *[Conflicts Q2 follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  I'd rather reassign it myself. First come, first served could put the wrong person on a job, for example someone without the right brand. So the system should suggest who is free, qualified, and has the lowest hours, and I pick. The person I assign then gets a notification.

**INTERVIEWER:  The requirements state that a staff member can reject a job. What information do you need from them, and what are the usual reasons?**   *[Conflicts Q3]*

**MANAGER:**  I need a reason. A short list works: personal emergency, clash with another job, not qualified or missing equipment, or other, with a comment box. The usual reasons are personal emergencies, or the previous job running late so the timing doesn't work. When they click reject, the system should warn them to talk to me first. It doesn't need my approval to go through, but the job goes back to unassigned, and I get notified straight away so I can reassign it. I'd like at least 48 hours' notice unless it's an emergency.

**INTERVIEWER:  Is telling the company ahead of time that you can't do a job the same as rejecting it?**   *[added follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  Yes, it's the same thing. Rejecting early just makes my life easier.

**INTERVIEWER:  How are unexpected breakdowns handled?**   *[Conflicts Q4]*

**MANAGER:**  The driver calls me. I mark the van as unavailable in the system for the days the workshop needs. Any jobs on that van become unassigned, and I either move them to another van or reschedule them with the customer. The crew moves to another van if one is short. Otherwise they're free for that day.

**INTERVIEWER:  In the event that a staff member goes past their assigned hours, for example overtime or a last-minute job, how will it be handled?**   *[Conflicts Q5]*

**MANAGER:**  If a job overruns, the crew finishes it and records the actual time when they mark it complete. That actual time counts in their workload. If I add a last-minute job to a crew's day, it counts too, and they get a notification. Anything above forty hours in the week shows up as overtime.

## **System**

**INTERVIEWER:  What are the metrics you would like to view? For example overall monthly jobs, total wages, total cost of jobs, how many hours each van has been used, or leave taken by each staff member.**   *[System Q1]*

**MANAGER:**  Wages and costs are handled by our accounts department, so I don't need those here. What I need is each staff member's hours this week and this month, the number of jobs done per month, leave taken and remaining for each person, and how many days each van was on the road.

**INTERVIEWER:  How would you rank them, in order of priority?**   *[System Q1 follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  Staff workload first, then unassigned jobs, then leave. Van usage is nice to have.

**INTERVIEWER:  What information do you need immediately on the landing page?**   *[System Q1 follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  This week's workload for every driver and technician, with anyone over forty hours highlighted, and the three lowest shown at the top. Below that, today's vans and crews, the number of jobs still unassigned, and any pending requests: leave, late availability changes and rejections.

**INTERVIEWER:  What would be the best way for you to view the availability and metrics? For example a calendar view, or the schedule overlaid with jobs.**   *[System Q2]*

**MANAGER:**  For availability, a calendar grid with staff down the side and days across, colour coded: available, morning only, afternoon only, and on leave. For the schedule, a weekly timeline with one row per van, showing the crew and their jobs. For workload, a simple bar chart per person with a line at forty hours. That's what I mean by seeing it at a glance.

**INTERVIEWER:  The requirements say you should be able to view up to three staff at once. Does each page show three staff, or can you select three staff to compare side by side?**   *[System Q3]*

**MANAGER:**  I select them. When I'm allocating a job, I want to pick up to three candidates and compare them side by side: their availability that day, their hours so far this week, their brand certifications, their preferences, and where they'll be that day, meaning the area of their other jobs. I don't need live GPS tracking, just where they're scheduled to be.

**INTERVIEWER:  When a staff member would go over 40 hours, or a technician is unqualified, or someone is double-booked, should the system stop you or just warn you?**   *[System Q4]*

**MANAGER:**  It depends on the rule. It should stop me for an unqualified technician, a double booking, someone on leave or not available, or a van without a valid crew. Those are never acceptable. It should only warn me for over forty hours, a preference not being met, or fewer than three vans in a day. Sometimes I have to accept those, so I want to be able to override the warning.

**INTERVIEWER:  How should staff find out about their assignments and any changes?**   *[added follow-up]*

**MANAGER:**  They should see them on their landing page, and get an email when the weekly schedule is published or something changes. For same-day changes, I'll still call them, because they might not check email while on a job.

## **Closing**

**INTERVIEWER:  Which of the needs we discussed matters most to you, and why?**

**MANAGER:**  Allocating jobs with valid crews, and seeing the workload at a glance. If a crew is wrong, a technician turns up and can't do the job, and that costs us the customer.

**INTERVIEWER:  Is there anything important we haven't covered?**

**MANAGER:**  I think that covers it. Just keep it simple. Most of my staff aren't very technical.

**INTERVIEWER:  Thank you for your time. We'll summarise your answers and send them to you to check.**

**MANAGER:**  Sounds good. Thanks.
