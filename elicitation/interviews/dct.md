# **Part B: Dual Certified Technician interview**

> **Scope redaction (2026-10-01, DEC-45):** the Answer-key summary was adjusted to the current scope. Remaining transcript wording is retained.

## **Opening**

**INTERVIEWER:  Hi, thank you for joining. We are the project team building the new job allocation system. This session is being recorded and transcribed on Zoom so we can capture your needs accurately. Is that okay with you?**

**TECHNICIAN:**  Yes, no problem.

**INTERVIEWER:  Could you briefly introduce yourself and your role?**

**TECHNICIAN:**  I'm one of the two technicians certified for both M Electric and Dicon. I've been with the company for about five years. I do installations and servicing for both brands, so I'm usually put on the vans that have jobs for both.

## **Clarifications**

**INTERVIEWER:  Can you be a driver as well?**   *[Clarification Q1]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  No, I only do the technical work. The drivers handle the driving, and I'm not covered by the company vehicle insurance anyway.

**INTERVIEWER:  When you give your availability, do you give whole days or specific hours?**   *[Clarification Q2]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  Half days, morning or afternoon. Most weeks I'm available Monday to Saturday, full days, so my pattern is pretty much the same every week. It would be nice to copy last week's availability instead of entering it all again.

**INTERVIEWER:  The requirements say "servicing or installing". Are you qualified in both, or only one of them?**   *[Clarification Q3]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  Both. The brand certification covers installation and servicing, and I have it for both brands.

**INTERVIEWER:  How long does each usually take? Is it a fixed timing for both?**   *[Clarification Q3 follow-up]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  Servicing is about an hour per unit, and installation is about three hours per unit. That's the standard, but it varies. A high-floor unit with long piping can take four or five hours, and an old, dirty unit takes longer to service. So a standard time is fine for planning, but I'd like to record the actual time.

**INTERVIEWER:  What are the job preferences that you would send?**   *[Clarification Q4]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  Since I'm certified for both brands, brand doesn't matter to me. My preferences are more about area and timing. I live in the east, so I prefer jobs in the east, and on Saturdays I prefer mornings only. I slightly prefer installations over servicing, but that's minor. I understand the manager can't always follow them.

## **Process**

**INTERVIEWER:  Walk me through the full process of how you would send your availability and accept job allocations.**   *[Process Q1]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  Right now, I message my availability to the manager on WhatsApp, and on Monday he sends next week's schedule in the group chat. With the new system, I'd enter my availability myself, and then see my jobs for the week on my landing page when he publishes on Monday. I don't need to accept each job one by one. If the schedule works, I just follow it. If there's a problem with one job, I'd reject that job.

**INTERVIEWER:  When do you usually send your availability? Has the Wednesday deadline ever been a problem?**   *[Process Q1 follow-up]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  I usually send it on Monday or Tuesday, so the deadline is fine most weeks. The problem is when something comes up after Wednesday, like a family matter. Right now I just message the manager and hope he sees it. It would be better to submit a request in the system, so it's recorded.

**INTERVIEWER:  How are you usually updated on your assignments? What would you prefer?**   *[Process Q1 follow-up]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  WhatsApp at the moment, which gets messy because the whole team is in one group. I'd prefer to see my own schedule in the system, and get an email or notification when it's published or changed. For a same-day change, a phone call is still best, because I might be in the middle of a job.

**INTERVIEWER:  What do you need to know about a job before you arrive? For example the address, customer contact, brand and model, or number of units.**   *[Process Q1 follow-up]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  The address with the unit number, the customer's name and phone number, the brand and model if they know it, how many units, whether it's installation or servicing, and the time slot. Notes are useful too, like where to park or whether there's a lift. If a customer has both brands at the same address, I'd like to see both jobs together.

**INTERVIEWER:  When you finish a job, what do you record or report?**   *[Process Q1 follow-up]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  Today I just message "done" in the group. In the system, I'd mark the job as complete, with the actual start and end time, a short remark, and whether a follow-up visit is needed, for example if we need to order a part.

**INTERVIEWER:  Since you are allowed to reject an assigned job, how do you determine whether a job can be done or not? What information do you need, and what is the full process?**   *[Process Q2]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  I check the time slot against my other jobs, including travel. I check that it's a brand I'm certified for, which is normally already correct. And for installations, I check that we have the right equipment and parts. The usual reasons I can't do a job are personal emergencies, or the job before it running late. The process would be: I call the manager first, then reject the job in the system and pick a reason.

**INTERVIEWER:  What happens after you reject?**   *[Process Q2 follow-up]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  The job should disappear from my list, and the manager gets told, so he can give it to someone else. I'd like a confirmation that the rejection went through, so I know I'm no longer expected there.

**INTERVIEWER:  How far ahead do you usually know that you cannot do a job?**   *[Process Q2 follow-up]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  Usually a few days ahead, if it's something planned. Emergencies are same day, of course.

**INTERVIEWER:  How do you apply for leave today, and how do you know how many days you have left?**   *[Process Q3]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  I fill in a leave form, or just message the manager. I actually don't know how many days I have left. I have to ask him. In the new system, I'd like to apply for leave directly and see how many of my seven days are left this year.

## **Conflicts**

**INTERVIEWER:  Do you usually get more jobs, or different jobs, than single-brand colleagues because of your dual certification?**   *[Conflicts Q1]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  Yes, more. I'm almost always on a mixed-brand van, and those are usually the busiest. I'm also the first person the manager calls to cover, because I can do either brand. Some weeks I end up close to forty hours, or over, while some single-brand colleagues have fewer. So I'd like the hours to be shared more fairly.

**INTERVIEWER:  What is the main difference between how you and single-brand colleagues are scheduled?**   *[Conflicts Q1 follow-up]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  They're placed on a van for their brand, so their week is quite predictable. I get moved around more. One day I'm the only technician on a mixed-brand van, the next I'm covering for someone. For big installations there's sometimes a second technician with me, but usually it's just me and the driver.

**INTERVIEWER:  When a colleague is unable to make it for a job, does the manager get your approval before reassigning you?**   *[Conflicts Q2]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  Not approval exactly. He usually calls me to check first, and then updates the schedule. I'm fine with that, as long as I get a notification in the system. If it would push me way over my hours, I'd like to be able to say no, the same way I'd reject any other job.

**INTERVIEWER:  A job overruns and affects your next assignment. What do you do?**   *[Conflicts Q3]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  I finish the current job first. The driver calls the next customer to say we're running late, and I let the manager know. If we'll be very late, he may move the next job to another van or reschedule it. At the end, I record the actual time, so the extra hours are counted.

## **System**

**INTERVIEWER:  What activities should be reflected in your allocated workload? For example travel, waiting, breaks or admin.**   *[System Q1]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  The job time and the travel between jobs. Lunch shouldn't count. Admin is small, just updating the job at the end, so it doesn't need its own time. Overtime should show clearly, so it's visible when I go over forty hours.

**INTERVIEWER:  What would help you understand your weekly assignments and monthly workload?**   *[System Q2]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  A weekly calendar showing my jobs each day, with times and addresses. Then my total hours for the week against forty, and a total for the month. My leave balance on the same page would be useful too.

**INTERVIEWER:  What device do you use to check the system during the day, and do you have reliable internet in the field?**   *[System Q3]*

**TECHNICIAN:**  My own phone, an Android. Mobile data is usually fine, but in basements and some condo car parks there's no signal. So I'd like to at least see today's jobs when I lose connection, even if I can only update them once I'm back online.

## **Closing**

**INTERVIEWER:  Which of the needs we discussed matters most to you, and why?**

**TECHNICIAN:**  Seeing my schedule clearly on my phone, and getting notified when it changes. Right now it's easy to miss a change in the group chat.

**INTERVIEWER:  Is there anything important we haven't covered?**

**TECHNICIAN:**  Just fair hours. There are only two of us who can do both brands, so it's easy for us to get overloaded.

**INTERVIEWER:  Thank you for your time. We'll summarise your answers and send them to you to check.**

**TECHNICIAN:**  Thanks.
